# Market and pricing (checked 2026-10-21)

Short-lived numbers: re-check prices and user counts before relying on them. This
note exists so the next session doesn't redo the research, and so the
"should this be a product" question is argued from data rather than mood.

## What competitors charge

| Product | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT Plus | about $20/month | consumer baseline |
| **Grok Bot** | **$30/month** Premium (Super $100, Ultra $200) | the product we're cloning |
| Self-hosted (this project) | about **$3–10/month** tokens + a VPS | DeepSeek Flash |

## What it costs us to run

- **Tokens:** about **$3–10 per user per month** at DeepSeek Flash prices for this
  workload (see `docs/PLAN.md`). A local model is about $0 — community users
  report running Hermes on a 12 GB GPU or on an Android phone.
- **Infrastructure:** one $5–30/month VPS comfortably serves **dozens** of light
  assistant users; the agent is idle between messages. Marginal cost per user is
  near zero.
- **The real costs are not compute:** onboarding, support, and (for customers)
  data handling and compliance. Third-party stories back this up — a GTM team
  cites saving **$800/month versus a Codex seat per person**, which means the
  value on offer is workflow, not tokens.

So gross margin on compute is roughly 90% at consumer pricing. The business is
won or lost on "does it work out of the box for a non-technical person" and "who
answers when it breaks", not on model choice.

## Is there demand?

Yes, and Hermes' own user-stories page is the evidence: **395 stories across 15
categories** (X, Reddit, GitHub, YouTube, blogs, Discord). The mix:

- **Business Ops: 57**, **Personal Assistant: 55**, **Enterprise: 23**,
  Integrations 35, Dev Workflow 82.
- A personal-assistant audience of 55 stories on a product pitched at developers
  is a strong signal: people are bending a dev tool into an executive assistant.

Concrete examples worth remembering:

- A team builds verified contact lists from one Slack message, on **one shared
  subscription** instead of a seat per person.
- A user built a local home-safety system with panic buttons for his 89-year-old
  grandfather.
- A prospect **phoned** a Hermes agent about a proposal and it closed the deal.
- A solo founder splits his startup across **seven** assistants.
- One user connected a whole business to a single Obsidian vault.

## The trap

The demand is for **DIY on free open source**. The platform layer is
commoditising toward free (Hermes, OpenClaw and the others are MIT and free), and
the big vendors are already in the consumer seat. A generic "another AI
assistant" has no moat.

What people are *not* doing is the boring part: a ready-made assistant that is
already wired to their email, calendar, lists and memory, already speaks
Telegram, and keeps their data on their own hardware. That is the defensible,
sellable thing — **vertical and turnkey, not general-purpose**. It is also
exactly what the task spine (`docs/TASK-SPINE.md`) is for.

## Suggested shape if it is sold

- **Price:** $20–100 per user per month, above the DIY floor (a user's time and a
  VPS) and at or below Grok Bot's $30 for the turnkey tier.
- **Model:** self-hosted single-tenant per customer (their VPS or ours), which is
  what the multi-tenant-ready rules in `docs/TASK-SPINE.md` protect.
- **Before selling anything:** the privacy caveat in `docs/PLAN.md` has to be
  solved (mail content currently goes to DeepSeek, a China-based provider) —
  route through a provider with clear data terms, or classify locally.
